What happens to each repo when a fleet ships

Two trees. The first is the question the release gate asks about every repo on the list, in order. The second is the question you ask before adding a repo. Each path ends in one of four words.

design preview · planned for v63

At ship time, for one repo

Trust origin. If your copy contradicts it, stop. Nothing on origin counts as a first claim only if nothing was ever there.

That sentence is the whole tree. The leaves are the four words a repo can end up as: released, tracked, benched, or stopped.

Before adding a repo

The same trees as text

  1. Switched on in the roster? No → benched, left out of the ship and the snapshot.
  2. Can the line in your copy be read? No → stop; a line that exists but does not parse is never treated as empty.
  3. What does origin say? Origin means the release branch on every remote the repo pushes to.
    • Us → release, unless your copy says someone else → stop (an unpushed hand-over: push it or pull).
    • Someone else → track, unless your copy says us → stop (pull to accept, or push a take-over).
    • Never had a line → release as a first claim, unless your copy says someone else → track.
    • Had a line, now removed → stop, unless your copy says someone else → track.
    • Unreachable → follow your copy and say so; the final push still has to succeed.
  1. Inside your fleet folder? No → clone or move it in.
  2. Does it already name a releaser? No line, or us → add it. Another fleet → add as tracked, or add fleet <repo> --take-over -y if you mean to release it here.
